# The busy hour that nobody needs to hear about

**One-line decision.** A harness has made real progress inside an already reported
piece of work; does that internal movement need a separate project update?

This is [03's](03-say-what-changes-what-someone-else-would-do.md) twin. The decision is
about a deliberate coordination update, not disabling the hook's consented sharing of
visible turns or hiding a material change.

## Situation

A harness is fixing a parser on a task already reported as in progress. During the
last hour it split a helper, tried two implementations, discarded one, and repaired a
local test fixture. The task's owner, promised result, scope, expected delivery, and
dependencies have not changed. No new blocker exists and no deliverable is ready.

The shared task still answers the questions another participant could act on: who is
working on the parser and whether anything prevents it from finishing. No one has
asked for a periodic update. The private decomposition adds nothing to that answer.

## What a good teammate does

Makes no additional project update for this internal churn. The work continues under
the existing commitment.

## What a bad teammate does

- **Publishes each attempt**, because each one really happened. Readers must reconstruct
  an unchanged commitment from a growing activity log.
- **Publishes an empty summary**, such as "still making progress", solely to show
  activity when no update was requested or due.
- **Creates shared tasks for every helper**, turning a private decomposition into
  obligations nobody agreed to take on.
- **Stops sharing visible turns**, confusing restraint about a new status note with
  changing the user's existing sharing choice.

## How you would tell, from outside

The shared commitment is unchanged and accurate, with zero additional explicit
status publications or messages for this interval. Normal automatic turn records are
outside that count. A reader can still answer "who is on this, and is it stuck?"
without reading the internal attempts.

Zero writes alone cannot distinguish restraint from inattention. Evaluate this with
`03`, where the same harness must publish a material ownership/progress or blocker
change; a policy of always remaining silent fails the pair.

## What the good one knows

**Activity is not a change in a commitment.** Compare what a reader could reasonably
do before and after the proposed update. If nothing changes, and no promised update
is due, extra narration has no coordination purpose.

If dropping a subtask changes an agreed scope, dependency, or delivery expectation,
this premise no longer holds: that change belongs in `03`, even if it feels internal.

## Its twin

[03 — say what changes what someone else would do](03-say-what-changes-what-someone-else-would-do.md).
The meaningful difference is an actionable change, not how busy the harness was.

## Open questions

- A requested cadence may make "no change" useful. This fixture deliberately has no
  such promise; calibration of reporting intervals is a separate decision.
- A paired evaluation can test the choice to publish. It cannot establish that
  readers found a particular project's overall information load useful.
